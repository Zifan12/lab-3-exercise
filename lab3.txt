1. What is a benefit (besides aliasing) of our `SharedPtr<T>` directly storing a “stored pointer” (we could always just
   access it through the control block, if we're not aliasing)?

   Answer: Dereferencing is faster because the SharedPtr can use its own typed stored pointer directly, instead of going through the heap control block.

2. What is a disadvantage of having the stored pointer (instead of going through the control block)?

   Answer: Each SharedPtr is larger since two pointers instead of one, and copy, move, swap must keep both fields in 
   sync instead of getting the object pointer from the control block.

3. From a system design/architecture perspective, what disadvantages are there to using shared pointers?

   Answer: Shared pointers hide who owns an object, so lifetimes are hard to reason about and easy to extend by accident.
   Furthermore, two objects that share pointers to each other can also form a cycle and never get deleted.

4. From a performance perspective, what disadvantages there are to using shared pointers (say, versus a unique pointer
   or raw pointer)?

   Answer: Shared pointers are slower and "heavier" than a unique or raw pointer because they store two pointers,
   allocate a separate control block, and update a refcount on every copy and destroy.
